From 23 trackers to one operating rhythm for receivables

B2B receivables across advertising, FMCG, D2C, cashback, government and bus operations were managed in separate trackers, with follow-ups tracked by hand. The leadership MIS, the customer notices and the collections follow-up all depended on them.

Challenge

No single version of the truth, committed collections slipping unseen until month end, and actions falling between people.

Approach

I spent two weeks timing every step before building anything, then redesigned each process and automated the check inside the tools the team already used.

Impact

Five working frameworks, adopted by the GM's team and replicated by a second team, and presented to all Revenue Assurance teams at the Automation Friday session.

Orvelle: designing the journey from first tap to repeat purchase

I wanted to see how one customer's path, from an Instagram ad to a checkout, flows into the numbers a business runs on. So I built a fashion brand: a store people use on their phones, and a Studio behind it that shows what those shoppers do as it happens.

The store

A home page that rearranges around the styles you pick, a style quiz, filters, saved items, a bag, phone login, a pincode delivery check and a checkout with UPI, cards, net banking and cash on delivery (payments simulated).

The Studio

Every visit, login, product view and order appears within seconds. It shows the journey funnel, who is in the store right now, and customers segmented by recency, frequency and value, each with a next best offer.

The operating model

A cohort model ties acquisition cost, repeat behaviour and returns to unit economics, and compares plan with actual orders. In the base case, 21% of a month's new buyers order again the next month.

Testing every invoice in a year, not a sample

A sample can show that a control exists, but not where it fails. I translated six procure-to-pay controls into rules with adjustable parameters, ran them in Python on the full population, and built a three-page Power BI dashboard.

Challenge

Duplicates, split invoices and self-approved payments in the untested population go unnoticed, and each is cash that has already left.

Approach

Six tests: duplicate payments, approval threshold avoidance, Benford's Law by vendor, three-way match, segregation of duties and after-hours posting. One exception row per invoice per failed test, with the reason.

Impact

1,152 unique exceptions (9.5%) and four of six tests above materiality. Threshold avoidance was largest: 315 invoices worth INR 12.9 Cr sit just under the approval limit or were split on the same day.

My recommendation was to route second approval on a vendor's cumulative spend per day, block duplicates in the ERP, and make a purchase order mandatory from INR 1 lakh. Tightening PO tolerance from 5% to 2% lifts exceptions from 1,152 to 1,610, which is why every threshold needs a written rationale.

What the work taught me

Four beliefs I now bring to every process, each one earned on a project above.

Time the process before you change it.

At Paytm I spent two weeks timing each step of receivables work before building anything. The delay was rarely where people thought it was, and the timings gave every conversation with a stakeholder a shared set of facts.

From the receivables engagement

Adoption is the deliverable.

A tool nobody opens saves nothing. The ticketing system gave every action one owner, an ETA and an escalation path. It worked because the GM's team used it, a peer intern could rebuild it, and a second team copied the reconciliation.

From the ticketing and reconciliation work

Let AI read; let rules and people decide.

My Gen AI reconciliation reads the emails, but partial payments follow written rules and unmatched emails go to a person. In CareerCompass, the answer to hallucination risk was guardrails plus a human-reviewed fallback.

From the capstone and CareerCompass AI

An exception is a lead, not a finding.

In the controls project, borderline vendors needed a reviewed outcome before they counted. Moving one threshold changed the exception count from 1,152 to 1,610, so each parameter is a judgement call that needs a reason on paper.
